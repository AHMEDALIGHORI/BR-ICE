You are building a premium, sellable brand 
website template: BR-ICE, a premium ice cream 
made from real milk with zero added sugar, sold 
to an international audience. Work as a senior 
web designer and creative technologist. Build it 
in phases, and stop and report at each

the Three.js scenes.
1. Concept
Brand placeholder: BR-ICE. The page is loud, 
colourful and Instagram-worthy, never childish: 
every flavour owns a flat, saturated colour, and 
each film floats on that colour so the page 
background can shift to it with no visible edge. 
Every product explodes: it starts whole and 
centred, then breaks apart in slow motion as you 
scroll (the cone cracks into shards, the scoop 
splits into slices that show the dense real-milk 
texture, the real ingredients burst out), and 
scrolling back up puts it together again. The 
story runs: the pint cracks open → the villain 
(sugar) → the hero (real milk) → the feel 
(texture) → six flavours blown apart → build your 
own cone → spotted around the world → shop. 
Tone of voice: confident, warm, a little cheeky 
("Everything you love. Nothing you don't." / "Your 
bag is empty. Bold of you.").
No text or logos in any image or video. Every 
word is HTML, so the brand can be renamed in 
the markup without regenerating a single asset. 
The asset prompts (second section of this pack) 
follow the same rule.

7. Quality bar
Scrubs are smooth in both directions with no 
black frames. The hero tags sit on their 
ingredients at 1440×900, 1024×768 and on 
phones.
GSAP writes an inline translate: none on 
anything it animates, so centring offsets for 
animated elements go through xPercent / 
yPercent, never CSS translate; hover effects 
go on a child, never on an element GSAP 
animates. The intro animates parent 
elements only, so it can never fight the scroll 
timelines.
Phones (≤760px): the hero film is 62% of the 
viewport height with feathered edges, BR 
above and ICE below the pint, and it shrinks 
as the pint bursts so the explosion fits; the 
theatre stacks film above copy.
prefers-reduced-motion: no smooth scroll, 
no pins or scrubs, end-frame posters, every 
section in its final readable state.
No console errors; WebGL failure falls back 
to a message, never a blank section.
